BAND E AMIR

Band-e Amir is one of Afghanistan’s most beautiful and important natural attractions, located in Bamyan Province in the central highlands of the Hindu Kush. It is famous for its six naturally formed turquoise-blue lakes: Band-e Zulfiqar, Band-e Pudina, Band-e Panir, Band-e Haibat, Band-e Qanbar, and Band-e Ghulaman. The lakes are situated at an elevation of around 2,900 meters above sea level and are separated by spectacular natural travertine dams formed over thousands of years. The surrounding landscape includes high mountains, rocky cliffs, valleys, grasslands, and diverse wildlife. Band-e Amir National Park covers approximately 600 square kilometers. In 2009, it was officially declared Afghanistan’s first national park. Visitors can enjoy breathtaking scenery, photography, hiking, boating, and exploring the natural landscape. With its crystal-clear blue waters, dramatic mountains, natural dams, and peaceful environment, Band-e Amir is one of Afghanistan’s most remarkable natural destinations.

Travel writer Agustinus Wibowo described Band-e Amir as one of the highlights of his journeys through Afghanistan, admiring its crystal-blue lakes surrounded by the barren mountains. Other travelers and writers, including Rory Stewart, have also written about the remarkable landscapes around Bamyan and Band-e Amir. Their accounts describe the area as a place where dramatic mountains, deep blue waters, and the rich cultural history of central Afghanistan come together, leaving a lasting impression on visitors from around the world.
